Lash Radar

An app that watches what clients are searching for and turns it into training. Trends, studio math and Texas rules in one place, built for the phone that sits next to the lash chair. Made for The Lash Collaboration, Kim and Bri's lash studio in Houston.

/ / The decisions

What I chose, and why.

A tool for licensed professionals has to earn trust before it earns screen time. These are the calls that shaped it.

The core idea / Trends drive the training

When clients start searching for a look, the training is already waiting.

Most lash training is written once and goes stale. Lash Radar connects to Google search data through the studio's own Google account and watches what people search for in the lash world. When a look starts rising, it shows up on the radar with its growth rate, a scope check, and a tutorial drafted for that trend.

The artists review the draft and make it their own, so the studio is teaching a trend while it's still climbing instead of after it peaks.

In this public demo the trends are sample data, labeled that way in the app. The live version runs on the studio's own Google account, so nothing here is connected to real search data.

01 / The phone in the chair

Built for one hand and a full appointment.

Lash artists work with both hands on a client. Everything is sized for a phone, installs to the home screen like an app, and keeps each job one tap away in its own tab.

02 / Rules from the source

No rule written from memory.

Every Texas rule in the Vault links to TDLR or the Texas Administrative Code and shows when it was checked. Anything the sources don't settle is flagged for a direct answer from TDLR or an attorney.

03 / Scope on every trend

A trend can be popular and still off limits.

Lash lifts and tints are outside the Texas eyelash extension license. Any trend that touches them carries a warning and links to the rule, so nobody books a service their license doesn't cover.

04 / Sample data says so

Demo numbers wear a label.

Until the studio's Google account is connected, the trend figures are samples. The app says so wherever they appear, so nobody mistakes a mockup for market research.

05 / Triage, not diagnosis

It sorts and refers out.

Eye reactions can't be undone. The triage tool helps an artist decide how urgent a reaction looks and who to send the client to. It never diagnoses and never suggests medication.

06 / Their craft, their call

The expertise belongs to the artists.

Application steps and technique notes are drafts for Kim and Bri to rewrite in their own words. Their courses stay out of this public version, and nothing in the app claims to be state approved training.

07 / Honest gaps

What it doesn't do yet.

It needs an internet connection, and checklists and logs save to each phone without syncing between artists. Both are on the list if the studio takes it into daily use.
